# ADR 0030: The 2.5D view of the Infrastructure architecture

Status: accepted (issue 320; builds on ADR 0028 and 0029)

## Context
The architecture is a good fit for a "network trace" look: depth with meaning, not decoration. WebGL or a 3D library would
break the page budget (SPEC W-1: no heavy JS frameworks, under 50 MB RSS server side) and the accessible baseline.

## Decision
- **Same renderer, a different skin.** The 2.5D view is the same SVG as the flat view, restyled with CSS only: raised slabs
  (stacked hard drop-shadows for the side face, one soft shadow), gradient top faces tinted by service kind, a faint tiled
  floor, glow only on live lines and failed parts. Entities, edges, order of focus, hit areas and text are identical, so
  selection, focus and lens survive a switch and a test compares the drawn edges with the declared graph in both styles.
- **Planes lift.** A lens (issue 316) lifts its plane in 2.5D: parts in the plane get a deeper side face and a glow, the rest
  dims. Nothing moves, so the layout stays stable (a rule of the focus view too). A full layered, tilted stack and a camera that
  moves into a layer are not built: they would need a different projection of the layout and would make hit areas and text
  harder to keep legible; the lift is the part of the idea that carries meaning.
- **Fallbacks.** The default is flat under `prefers-reduced-motion` and on a phone (under 640 px); the switcher (issue 324) turns
  2.5D on there. Status is never colour alone in either style (shape, text, dashed outline).

## Measured cost
Headless Chromium 1920 x 1080 (software rendering, so a pessimistic number), the page animating a burst of 15 dots every 0.7 s
for 8 s, mean main-thread busy time and heap:

| nodes | style | main thread busy | JS heap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | flat | 23 % of one core | 3.3 MB |
| 6 | 2.5D | 19 % | 3.2 MB |
| 50 | flat | 34 % | 3.6 MB |
| 50 | 2.5D | 36 % | 3.5 MB |

2.5D costs nothing measurable beyond the flat view; the animation itself is the load, and it stops at governor L3 and when the page
is hidden. Server-side RSS is unchanged (the change is in one static file).

## Consequences
- The filters are paint-time work: a very large node count with many simultaneous dots is where it would show first; the overview
  (ADR 0029) is the answer for large clusters.
